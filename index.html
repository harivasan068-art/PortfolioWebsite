    Energy-Aware Cluster-Based Routing Protocol for Underwater Communication Networks

    Author / Presenter: Harivasan V • Affiliation: Rajalakshmi Institute of Technology • Track: Green IoT & Acoustic Communications
    +28% Energy Conservation
    +19% Packet Delivery Ratio
    -32% End-to-End Latency
    100% Acoustic Multi-Hop

    Abstract

    Underwater Acoustic Sensor Networks (UASNs) play a critical role in oceanographic data collection, pollution monitoring, disaster prevention, and tactical surveillance. However, acoustic communication is severely hindered by limited battery capacities, high propagation delays (~1500 m/s), low available bandwidth, and frequent node mobility caused by water currents. This paper proposes a novel Energy-Aware Cluster-Based Routing Protocol (EACR-UW) tailored specifically to address these underwater challenges.

    Key Architectural Innovations

    • Dynamic Residual Energy Weighting: Cluster Head (CH) election calculates a composite fitness score combining current residual battery energy, distance to the surface sink buoy, and neighbor node density.
    • Adaptive Duty Cycling: Idle listening energy is curtailed by dynamically putting non-forwarding sensor nodes into sleep cycles synchronized with acoustic transmission windows.
    • Multi-Hop Inter-Cluster Acoustic Relays: Instead of costly direct high-power transmissions to distant surface buoys, packets hop across optimally positioned CHs, slashing aggregate energy drain by 28%.
    • Current-Resilient Clustering: Cluster maintenance intervals dynamically adapt to node mobility drift rates, preserving topology stability in fluctuating water columns.

    Results & Impact

    Comprehensive NS-3 / Aqua-Sim simulation benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed protocol outperforms conventional LEACH-UWSN and DEEC-UW benchmarks with a 28% extension in overall network operational lifetime and a 19% improvement in packet delivery ratio (PDR) under severe underwater multipath fading conditions.
